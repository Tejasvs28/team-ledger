MVP

Identifies who contributed most to winning, not just who racked up the biggest numbers. Formula shown below — nothing hidden.

How This Is Calculated

Small-sample correction: before anything else, every player's batting rate, bowling rate, and win-contribution rate is blended with the squad average for that category, weighted by how many innings/overs/wins they actually have (same shrinkage logic as Recommended Batting Position). A player with 1-2 games can't post an extreme rate stat and jump straight to the top purely on a small sample — their number gets pulled toward the team average until they've built up around 5-8 innings/overs of real track record. Watch for the ⚠ marker in the Sample column below.

Batting (40%): Average + pitch-adjusted Strike Rate÷2, scaled relative to the best in your squad. Average uses your real runs; Strike Rate is adjusted so a baseball-diamond innings (naturally lower-scoring) is compared fairly against a turf/matting innings (naturally higher-scoring) — e.g. SR 65 on a baseball diamond scales to roughly SR 92 in adjusted terms.

Bowling (40%): Wickets×15 − pitch-adjusted Economy×10, same reasoning applied symmetrically — containing batters is harder on a high-scoring turf pitch than a low-scoring baseball one.

All-Round Bonus (10%): the lower of your batting and bowling scores — rewards being genuinely strong in both, not just one.

Team Impact (10%): % of the team's wins where this player had a "big contribution" (30+ runs, 2+ wickets, or 2+ fielding contributions). This — not a blanket win/loss multiplier — is deliberately the only place win/loss result affects the score. An excellent performance in a losing effort is still scored as excellent; it just doesn't earn Team Impact points the way the same performance in a win would.

Fielding is not part of this score — how many catches come your way depends a lot on where the ball gets hit, not just skill, so it's ranked separately below rather than dragging the overall MVP number up or down.

What this still can't see: opponent strength, phase-specific bowling (powerplay vs death), how difficult a specific catch was, or true ball-by-ball pressure. Scores are relative to your own squad this season, not a universal rating.
